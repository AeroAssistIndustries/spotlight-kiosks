How to get more Google reviews (without breaking the rules)

A system that asks every customer, every time — and why that's better than any incentive.

In this guide

  2. What's allowed and what isn't
  3. Build the ask into the visit
  4. Reply to every one
  5. When a review breaks the rules
  6. Beyond Google

Reputation · 6 min read

All guides

The math of reviews

A business with a 4.7 rating and 300 recent reviews beats a 4.9 with 40 old ones, in Google's ranking and in the customer's mind. Recency and volume signal that you're busy and consistent. The only reliable way to get both is to ask everyone, every time, automatically.

What's allowed and what isn't

  • Allowed: asking customers to leave a review, sending a direct link, putting a QR code on the counter, asking in person.
  • Not allowed: paying or discounting for reviews, asking only happy customers (“review gating”), writing reviews yourself or through friends, buying reviews. Google removes these and can suspend the profile; the FTC fines for it.

Build the ask into the visit

  1. Get the direct review link from your Google Business Profile (“Ask for reviews”).
  2. Send it automatically after each visit — a text 2–4 hours later works best for restaurants and services; a next-day email for hotels and practices.
  3. Keep the message short and personal: “Thanks for coming in today, Maria. If you have a minute, a Google review helps us more than you'd think: [link]”.
  4. Add a QR code card at the counter, on the table or on the invoice for people who'd rather do it now.
  5. Train staff to mention it once, at the natural moment (checkout, check-out, job complete).

Reply to every one

Replies are read by the next customer more than by the reviewer. Thank the good ones specifically (“glad the patio worked for the birthday”). For negative ones: acknowledge, don't argue, take it offline (“please call me directly at…”), and fix the thing if it's real. A calm reply to a bad review is the most persuasive content on your profile.

When a review breaks the rules

Reviews that are spam, off-topic, from a competitor or a non-customer, or that contain hate or personal information can be flagged for removal in the profile. It works more often than people think, but it takes days to weeks — reply in the meantime.

Beyond Google

Google first, then the platform your industry lives on: TripAdvisor for hotels, Yelp for restaurants, Healthgrades for practices, Facebook for local services. Alternate your requests so you build all of them.
